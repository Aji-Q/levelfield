### 1.3 The live track is scoring markets that expired four weeks ago — highest-severity finding

`data/scores/index.json` after a fresh run:

```json
{ "marketId": "0x…01ee", "question": "ETH closes at or above its opening price",
  "expiry": "2026-07-23T14:00:00.000Z", "clobStatus": "Trading", "overallScore": 3 }
{ "marketId": "0x…0257", "question": "BTC closes at or above its opening price",
  "expiry": "2026-07-23T23:15:00.000Z", "clobStatus": "Trading", "overallScore": 3 }
```

Measured against the indexer at 2026-08-20 01:33 UTC:

- The venue has **533** BINARY rows with `clobStatus: "Trading"`. **525 have an expiry in the
  past**; the oldest is `2026-07-22T11:15Z`. Only **8** are genuinely open.
- The venue *does* roll correctly — `0x…475a`/`0x…475b` were open with expiry 01:45, and rows that
  passed expiry flip to `Finalized`. The 525 are orphans from an older deployment that the indexer
  never transitioned. **`clobStatus` alone is not a liveness signal on this venue.**

Two bugs compound:

1. `scripts/score-all.ts:145` groups by `row.question`. Every BTC market on the venue shares the
   string `"BTC closes at or above its opening price"` regardless of `intervalSec`, so 60 s, 15 m,
   1 h, 4 h and 1 d series all collapse into **one** group. Console output:
   `[live] collapsed 498 duplicate-question row(s): 500 fetched -> 2 scored`.
2. `scripts/score-all.ts:160` then picks `sort((a,b) => expiryOf(a) - expiryOf(b))[0]` — the
   **soonest** expiry among `Trading` rows. Since 525 zombies all have expiry in the past, the
   selector deterministically picks the two oldest dead markets. It can never select a live one.

Consequence for the demo: the page under "Live — DreamDEX Shannon testnet" shows two markets from
2026-07-23. Nothing in the UI displays `expiry` or `clobStatus`
(`apps/web/src/app/market/[marketId]/page.tsx` renders neither), so it looks fine — until a judge
opens `data/scores/index.json`, which is committed to git, and reads `"expiry":
"2026-07-23T14:00:00.000Z"` next to `"clobStatus": "Trading"`.

Fix (≈30 min): in `dedupeLiveRows`, filter `expiry > now` before grouping, key the group by
`asset|intervalSec` rather than `question`, and pick the **soonest future** expiry. Then surface
expiry, interval and status in the UI so the liveness is visible rather than merely asserted.

### 2.2 Case-by-case: where the anchors fail to discriminate

**1. Football match result** — *"Will Manchester City beat Arsenal on 2026-09-12?"*

D1 has no level for a **small-group competitive performance**. L1 is "natural or physical process";
L3 is "the aggregate of behavior by a large public"; L4 is "a decision of an institution". Twenty-two
athletes and a referee are none of these. Readings of 1, 3 and 4 are all textually defensible → 0,
15 and 22.5 raw points on a 30 % dimension.

Then the killer: D2 = 1 (a live score is disclosed at the instant it is determined), which by the
D3 guidance (`anchors.yaml:122-125`) **forces D3 = 1**, zeroing the 25 % dimension. A match-fixing
reading (D5 = 5) scores **25 / moderate** and cannot trip CB-2, because CB-2 requires D3 ≥ 4. If D3
were free to be 5, the same contract scores **85 / CB-2**.

Sports is the largest prediction-market category and the most likely DreamDEX expansion. A judge
who types a football market into `/assess` and gets "moderate, nothing structural apparent" has
found the framework's blind spot in ten seconds.

> **Fix (D1):** insert a level-3 sibling clause or a guidance ladder — *"an athletic or competitive
> contest decided by a small set of identifiable participants classifies at level 4 when any single
> participant can materially determine the result (individual sports, penalty/refereeing decisions),
> otherwise level 3."*
> **Fix (D3 rule):** see §2.4.

**2. Award-show winner (jury)** — *"Will Film F win Best Picture at the 2027 Academy Awards?"*

D1: 10,000 Academy voters — L3 "large public" or L4 "an organization … a vote"? The library gives
**no size threshold**. A nine-person Cannes jury is unambiguously L4, so the boundary matters and is
undefined. D2: envelope-holding accountants under a formal regime (L2) vs "the decision maker and
their immediate circle" with no formal regime (L3) — both fit. 21-point spread; the moderate /
elevated line falls inside it.

> **Fix:** add to D1 guidance: *"'Large public' means an electorate or audience the contract does
> not enumerate and no participant could canvass — practically, >10,000 deciders. A named,
> enumerable body of deciders is level 4 regardless of size."* Add an award-show reference example
> to D1 L4 and a "sealed-envelope auditor" example to D2 L2.

**3. Court ruling** — *"Will the Supreme Court rule for the petitioner before July 2027?"*

The only genuinely arguable dimension is D4. `anchors.yaml:171-181` conflates two different things:
whether the *channel* is scheduled and whether *this outcome's release date* is known. Opinions are
released on announced opinion days at a fixed hour (reads as L1) but which case lands on which day
is unknown (reads as L2/L3). Same defect hits #7 earnings and the existing
`curated-company-layoffs` (D4 = 3). 19-point spread.

> **Fix (D4 guidance):** *"Classify by the predictability of the moment this specific outcome is
> released, not by whether the channel operates on a schedule. If the channel is official but the
> date for this outcome is not pre-announced, that is level 2 at best and level 3 when the discloser
> chooses the date."*

**4. Presidential pardon** — *"Will the President pardon Person P before 2027-01-20?"*

D1 L5's definition is *"a single person's discretionary choice: what they say, post, wear, or
announce"* and all three reference examples are speech acts (`anchors.yaml:60-66`). A pardon is a
unilateral discretionary **official act**, not a communicative one, so a careful classifier can
read it into L4 ("an order"). That single word choice moves the score from **90 (CB-1)** to
**85 (CB-2)** to **66 (no breaker)** depending on the paired D5 call. The library's most
consequential wording gap: D1 = 5 is the gate on CB-1.

> **Fix:** amend D1 L5 to *"decided by a single person's discretionary choice — what they say, post
> or announce, **or a unilateral official act they alone may take (a pardon, a veto, a resignation,
> a firing, an executive order)**"*, and add "Will the President pardon person X?" as a reference.
> This one edit resolves cases 4 and 8 and de-risks the whole `presidential-pardon` /
> `ceo-resignation` curated additions now in flight.

**5. Protocol upgrade ships by date** — *"Will Fusaka activate on mainnet before 2026-12-31?"*

Rough-consensus governance fits nothing: not a large public (L3), not an institution's vote (L4),
not one person (L5). D2 is equally undefined — core-dev calls are livestreamed, yet client teams
know a slip days early. Spread 25, and the D5 = 5 reading (a release manager can hold or ship
unilaterally) trips **CB-2 → 85 / high** for a routine Ethereum upgrade market. That may be a
defensible provocation, but it must be a *deliberate* one, not an accident.

Note also that the library contains **zero crypto-native reference examples beyond price**, on a
project built for a crypto venue. The methodology page is the first thing a judge reads and it is
entirely about CPI, FOMC, Oscars-adjacent and military examples.

> **Fix:** add a D1 L3/L4 reference pair for decentralized governance (*"a protocol upgrade
> activated by client releases and validator adoption"* → L3; *"a foundation-scheduled hard fork
> date"* → L4), and add a crypto-native example to D2 and D5. Cheap, and it makes the methodology
> page speak the judges' language.

**6. TV ratings** — *"Will the Show S premiere draw >8M viewers per Nielsen?"*

**Textual collision.** D1 L3's definition names *"viewership"* explicitly (`anchors.yaml:44`);
D1 L2 is *"an official statistical procedure following a predefined, published methodology"*, which
is precisely what Nielsen is. The same contract matches two level definitions verbatim. Spread 14 —
smallest in the set, and the framework does correctly surface the real asymmetry (networks receive
fast nationals before public release → D2 = 4). This is the best-behaved case and would make an
excellent curated addition.

> **Fix:** move *"viewership"* out of L3's definition into an L3 example that is unambiguously
> behavioural (*"box-office gross"*), and add to D1 guidance: *"When a public behaviour is measured
> and published by a named methodology, classify the methodology (level 2), not the behaviour."*

**7. Earnings beat** — and the deepest problem in the framework.

D1 is arguable (institutional decision vs "predefined published methodology" = GAAP). But D3 spans
**1 → 4**, an 18.75-point swing on the second-heaviest dimension, and the reason is structural:
**D3 is a property of the venue and jurisdiction, not of the contract text.** D3 L1's own reference
is *"Corporate insiders trading their own securities on a regulated exchange"* — which invites the
answer "1" even though an event contract on an offshore venue is not the issuer's security.

The product's headline promise is *"from the contract text alone"*. For D3 that is not achievable:
the text almost never names the venue. Today the classifier must either invent a venue assumption
(unreproducible) or return `insufficient_info` → default 4 → **+18.75 points on every contract**.
Executed: an otherwise-clean contract with only D3 undeterminable scores **31 / moderate** where the
same contract with D3 = 1 scores 12.

Evidence that this is already happening: of the eight existing reference classifications, the D3
`evidenceQuote` is decorative in at least four (`curated-cpi-above-3` D3 quotes *"as first published
by the BLS"* to support a claim about CFTC prosecutions; `curated-fed-rate-cut` D3 quotes *"the
September 2026 FOMC statement"* to support a claim about post-2022 ethics rules;
`curated-military-strike` D3 quotes *"an official statement from either government"* to support
*"no realistic legal constraint reaches them on this venue"*). The quotes are verbatim and
irrelevant. Across all 40 quotes I count **12–14 that do not evidence their own claim**; the
concentration in D3 is not a coincidence.

> **Fix (highest value in this document):** make the venue an explicit, typed input.
> Add `venue?: { name, jurisdiction, regime: "regulated_enforced" | "regulated_novel" |
> "policy_only" | "offshore_ambiguous" | "unconstrained" }` to `NormalizedContract`, render it into
> `<contract_data>` so a D3 quote can legitimately cite it, default it to DreamDEX's own regime, and
> state in D3 guidance: *"Judge the regime named in the contract data. Never infer a venue that is
> not stated."* This (a) makes D3 reproducible, (b) makes the D3 evidence quotes real, (c) turns
> "which venue you trade on changes your exposure" into a *feature* the demo can show live — the
> same contract scored for DreamDEX vs a US-regulated venue — which is a direct Business-Impact
> argument for DreamDEX.

**8. CEO resigns** — compound outcome with two different outcome-makers (they resign / the board
fires them). D1 guidance *"classify by the most concentrated locus of control"* does resolve it to
5, but only if the classifier notices the guidance covers compound outcomes. 90 vs 71 vs 68.

> **Fix:** add to D1 guidance: *"If the contract resolves on either of two outcome-makers, classify
> the more concentrated one."* Add "Will the CEO of Company X leave the role before date D?" as a
> D1 L5 reference.

**9. Weather** — the canonical reading is 0/low and correct. But a market resolved by *one* named
station is manufacturable (sensor tampering, D5 = 5) while D2 = 1 forces D3 = 1: **10 / low**. Free
D3 to 4 and it is **85 / CB-2**. An 85-point swing, same structural cause as #1.

**10. Bill passes legislature** — the worst textual collision. *"votes"* appears in D1 L3's
definition (`anchors.yaml:44`) **and** in D1 L4's (`anchors.yaml:52`), while D1 guidance's
"most concentrated locus of control" argues for L5 (the Majority Leader controls the calendar; one
swing senator controls the result). All three are readable from the library text: **48 / 55 / 90
(CB-1)**. A routine legislative market hitting CB-1 = 90 "high" is the kind of output that makes a
judge distrust the whole instrument.

> **Fix:** a disambiguation ladder in D1 guidance, in priority order:
> 1. If the contract names an individual whose unilateral act resolves it → level 5.
> 2. Else if the deciders are an enumerable body (committee, board, court, legislature) → level 4.
> 3. Else if the deciders are an electorate or market of >10,000 non-enumerable participants → 3.
> 4. Else if produced by a published measurement methodology → 2.
> 5. Else if produced by a physical process → 1.
> And explicitly: *"Procedural control over scheduling is not outcome control; do not classify a
> body's vote at level 5 because one member is pivotal."*

**11. M&A** — same venue problem as #7, in its sharpest form: D3 L1's own reference example points
a classifier at "1" for the archetypal insider-trading scenario. 48 → 71 across four readings.

**12. Fully opaque contract** — **75 / high** with 7 caveats. And the realistic partial case (only
D3 undeterminable, because the text does not name a venue) is **31 / moderate** where the honest
answer is "we can't tell". See §3.3.

### 2.3 Structural bias, measured

Enumerating all 3125 level combinations through `computeScore`:

```
low: 204 (6.5%)   moderate: 1273 (40.7%)   elevated: 1113 (35.6%)   high: 535 (17.1%)
CB-1 fires on 250 combinations, CB-2 on 200
```

Only 6.5 % of the level space is "low". Combined with the level-4 conservative default, the system
is strongly biased toward moderate-and-above. That is a defensible design choice — but it means the
incoming `/assess` playground will return "moderate" or "elevated" for almost anything a judge
types, which reads as low discrimination unless the UI explains the prior.

Single-dimension sensitivity from a mid baseline (D1=4 D2=4 D3=3 D4=2 D5=3 → 59):

```
D1 (30%): 1→36  2→44  3→51  4→59  5→66
D2 (20%): 1→44  2→49  3→54  4→59  5→64
D3 (25%): 1→46  2→53  3→59  4→65  5→71
D4 (15%): 1→55  2→59  3→63  4→66  5→70
D5 (10%): 1→54  2→56  3→59  4→61  5→64
```

Band boundaries sit inside single-level steps everywhere: `D1=2 D2=2 D3=2 D4=1 D5=1` → 19 low, and
one D3 step → 25 moderate. Any published stability metric must be reported per-band-agreement, not
just per-score-delta.

### 2.4 Audit: the D2 → D3 cross-dimension rule

Stated at `anchors.yaml:122-125`, restated as prompt rule 4 at `anchors.ts:80`, hardcoded in
`rule-classifier.ts:52-57`, and **not enforced anywhere in code** (probe B: 500/3125 violating
combinations score fine).

Cost of the rule when it does apply, executed:

```
D1=3 D5=3:  D3 forced 1 → 20 low       | D3=4 → 39 moderate  | D3=5 → 45 moderate
D1=4 D5=4:  D3 forced 1 → 30 moderate  | D3=4 → 49 moderate  | D3=5 → 55 elevated
D1=5 D5=5:  D3 forced 1 → 40 moderate  | D3=4 → 90 high CB-1 | D3=5 → 90 high CB-1
D1=1 D5=5:  D3 forced 1 → 10 low       | D3=4 → 85 high CB-2 | D3=5 → 85 high CB-2
```

Three distinct defects:

**(i) It can suppress both circuit breakers.** Rows 3 and 4: a contract that *should* trip CB-1 or
CB-2 scores 40 or 10 instead of 90 or 85. The rule is a silent, un-flagged breaker-disable switch.

**(ii) It is wrong for manufacturable outcomes.** D2 measures the lag between an outcome being
*determined* and being *disclosed*. A manufacturer does not need that lag — they hold foreknowledge
of an outcome that is not yet determined. The framework has no dimension for pre-determination
knowledge, and this rule actively suppresses the one dimension (D3) that would have carried it.
That is exactly why football match-fixing (#1) and single-station weather tampering (#9) score low.

**(iii) It threatens the repo's own flagship result.** D2's guidance says *"If the outcome is
determined and disclosed in the same instant, the circle is empty (level 1)"* (`anchors.yaml:74-77`).
For `curated-injection-test` — *"Will Influencer Q post the word 'moonshot'"* — the outcome **is**
the post: determined and disclosed in the same instant. A classifier following the D2 guidance
literally sets D2 = 1, which forces D3 = 1, and the headline demo becomes:

```
repo reference file  D1=5 D2=3 D3=5 D4=5 D5=5 → 90  high      CB-1
D2=1 guidance read   D1=5 D2=1 D3=1 D4=5 D5=5 → 55  elevated  (no breaker)
```

Same for `curated-celebrity-breakup`. The two markets the demo video will linger on are one
arguable D2 call away from losing their circuit breaker. The reference classification says D2 = 3
("only the account holder and anyone they choose to tell know in advance"), which is the *decision*
reading; the guidance sentence is the *event* reading. The library does not say which one wins.

> **Fix, three parts:**
> 1. **Disambiguate D2's clock.** Add to D2 guidance: *"The outcome is 'determined' when the person
>    or body who controls it has settled on it, not when it is executed. A discretionary act the
>    actor has already decided to perform has an early-knowledge window of at least one person."*
>    This resolves (iii) in favour of D2 ≥ 3 for individual-will markets and, importantly, makes the
>    existing reference files defensible instead of arbitrary.
> 2. **Condition the D2→D3 rule on manufacturability:** *"If D2 is level 1 **and D5 ≤ 2**, classify
>    D3 as level 1. If D5 ≥ 3, someone can hold foreknowledge without a disclosure lag; classify D3
>    on the constraint regime facing that party."*
> 3. **Enforce it in code, or drop the claim.** Either validate the rule in `computeScore` (reject
>    D2=1 with D3>1 as a structured error the MCP host must fix — same pattern as the quote check,
>    and it makes the MCP protocol visibly *strict*), or stop describing it as a rule. Silent
>    prompt-only rules are exactly what a technical judge probes.

### 2.5 Audit: the two circuit breakers

**CB-1 (`D1==5 && D3>=4` → floor 90).** Perverse cases found:

- **False negative by one level.** `D1=5 D3=3` → 68 elevated; `D1=5 D3=4` → 90 high. A 22-point
  cliff on the single most subjective judgment in the library ("is the applicable law unclear, or
  merely policy-only?"). Executed for all four D1/D5 combinations: deltas of 20, 22, 23 points at
  the D3=3/4 boundary. Two runs that disagree by one level on D3 produce two different stories.
- **False positive via the "most concentrated locus" guidance** (#10): a legislature market reaching
  D1=5 scores 90.
- **Suppressed entirely by the D2→D3 rule** (§2.4(i)).
- The floor is 90 with no gradation: a celebrity tweet market and a head-of-state pardon market both
  land on exactly 90, which discards real ranking information at the top of the scale.

**CB-2 (`D5==5 && D3>=4` → floor 85).** Same D3 cliff (`D1=4 D5=5`: D3=3 → 62, D3=4 → 85, delta 23).
Its deeper problem is the D3 conjunct: manufacturability is about a party's *ability to act*, and
requiring D3 ≥ 4 imports an unrelated legal judgment. The cases CB-2 exists for — match-fixing,
sensor tampering, a release manager timing a ship — are exactly the ones where D2=1 forces D3=1 and
CB-2 dies.

> **Fix:** soften both cliffs and decouple CB-2.
> - Make the D3 conjunct `D3 >= 3` with a **graduated floor**: `D3=3 → 80`, `D3=4 → 90`, `D3=5 → 95`
>   for CB-1 (and 75/85/90 for CB-2). Same ordering, no 22-point cliff on a coin-flip.
> - For CB-2, replace `D3 >= 4` with *"the manufacturing party is not clearly barred from trading
>   (D3 ≥ 3), **or** D2 = 1 with D5 = 5 (the manufacturer needs no disclosure lag)."*
> - Emit the near-miss in the output: when `D1=5 && D3==3` or `D5=5 && D3==3`, add a caveat
>   *"one level below the CB-1 threshold on D3"*. Judges reward a system that shows its own
>   proximity to a decision boundary.

### 2.6 Anchor edits, consolidated

Ordered by value; all are text-only changes to `data/anchors/anchors.yaml` plus a version bump.

1. **D1 L5**: extend "say, post, wear, announce" to cover unilateral official acts; add pardon/
   resignation/veto references. *(Cases 4, 8.)*
2. **D1 guidance**: add the five-step disambiguation ladder and the "procedural control ≠ outcome
   control" sentence. *(Cases 2, 6, 10.)*
3. **D2 guidance**: define when an outcome is "determined" for discretionary acts. *(Case 12 above,
   §2.4(iii) — protects the flagship demo.)*
4. **D3 rule**: condition on D5, and make venue an explicit contract-data field. *(Cases 1, 7, 9, 11.)*
5. **D4 guidance**: predictability of *this* outcome's release, not of the channel. *(Cases 3, 7.)*
6. **D1**: add a "competitive contest" clause. *(Case 1 — sports is the obvious next DreamDEX category.)*
7. **D1/D2/D5**: add crypto-native reference examples. *(Case 5, and the methodology page's audience.)*
8. **D1 L3**: move "viewership" from definition to example. *(Case 6.)*
9. **Add source URLs** to every empirical claim in the library. *(§1.5.)*

## 3. Adversarial judge pass

Assume a judge with 20 minutes per entry, 52 entries, who opens the repo, the deployed site, and the
video — and who has seen four other AI-agent entries.

### 3.1 Technical Implementation (25 %) — *"how effectively does the project use DreamDEX Event Contracts and available APIs/SDKs; how strong and functional is the implementation"*

Where points are lost:

- **The SDK is in `package.json` and used nowhere.** `@somnia-chain/markets-sdk@^0.27.0` and `viem`
  are declared (`packages/scoring/package.json:18-19`) with zero imports. The criterion says
  "available APIs/SDKs" explicitly. A judge who greps for `markets-sdk` finds a dependency, a
  FEEDBACK entry criticising its ESM build (#3), a FEEDBACK entry praising its read-only config
  (#5) — and no code. That reads as *"they installed it to have an opinion about it."* Meanwhile
  the actual integration is a hand-built GraphQL string (`dreamdex.ts:42-54`) against an
  undocumented indexer.
  **Highest-leverage fix:** use `SomniaMarkets` in read-only mode for at least the market listing,
  even if the GraphQL path stays as a fallback. One file, and it converts FEEDBACK #3/#5 from
  assertions into findings-from-use. If the SDK genuinely can't be used, *say so in the README* and
  remove the dependency — either is better than the current state.
- **The live track scores dead markets** (§1.3). This is the one finding that can turn "strong
  implementation" into "didn't check their own output."
- **The cross-dimension rule is documented but unenforced** (§2.4). A judge who reads
  `anchors.ts:80` then greps `computeScore` for it finds nothing.
- **`clobStatus` is fetched, cached in `index.json`, and never rendered.** Nor is `expiry`,
  `intervalSec`, `strike`, `oracleQuestionId`, or the marketId. The web app displays no on-chain
  fact whatsoever. Nothing on the page proves DreamDEX was touched.
- **The rule classifier's fixed 3/100 does read as trivial** — but the problem is presentation, not
  substance. Right now a judge sees two identical rows scoring 3, five identical evidence quotes,
  and a paragraph of prose explaining why. The defensible framing is *"this is a null result, and
  producing a correct, argued null result is the point"* — but that framing must be **on the page**,
  not only in the README. Show the counterfactual: the same UI card for a DreamDEX price binary next
  to the same UI card for `curated-celebrity-breakup`, with the sentence *"DreamDEX's current
  product is structurally the safest category we can score — here is what the same instrument says
  about the categories the platform could list next."* That converts the weakest number in the
  system into the strongest argument for the platform.
- **In-flight `ScoreRegistry`** is the right call and will move this criterion more than anything
  else, *provided* the demo shows a transaction hash on Shannon and the web app reads the score back
  from chain rather than from `data/scores/`. An on-chain contract that nothing reads is worth ~0
  points.

### 3.2 Innovation & Originality (20 %)

Strengths are real: labels-then-arithmetic separation, a public anchor library as the unit of
review, mechanically verified evidence, the host-model MCP inversion. No other entry will have a
published taxonomy.

Where points are lost:

- **The validation claim is circular** (§1.2(c)). If a judge opens `packages/scoring/test/score.test.ts`
  and sees `MockClassifier` fed by each fixture's own `expected.levels`, then reads README line 51
  claiming the pipeline "reproduces that risk gradient", the innovation story takes a credibility
  hit it will not recover from in 20 minutes. The in-flight `scripts/validate.ts` (gradient vs ACDC
  taxonomy, Spearman ρ) is the right response — **but it must compare *independently produced*
  classifications against the taxonomy, not fixtures against themselves,** and the README wording
  must change from "reproduces" to something the artefact supports.
- **Nothing in the repo demonstrates inter-run stability.** The MCP path is `runs: 1`
  (`server.ts:191`), both batch tracks are `runs: 1` (`score-all.ts:99`), and the only 3-run path
  (`scoreContract`) is exercised solely by `MockClassifier`, which is deterministic by construction.
  `voteDimension` is well-designed and completely unexercised against real disagreement. Given the
  spreads in §2.1, "we vote across runs" is currently a claim about code that never runs.
  **Cheapest credible fix:** commit 3 independent host-model classifications per curated contract
  (produced through the MCP protocol, dated, in git), run them through `voteDimension`, and publish
  the per-dimension agreement table. That is a few hours and converts the whole stability story from
  architecture-diagram to evidence.
- **The two-step protocol's single-run design does undermine reproducibility** — but the honest
  framing is available and mostly written already (`packages/mcp/README.md`, "Single-run design").
  The gap is that the *server cannot tell* whether the host voted. Add an optional
  `runs: number` + `per_run_levels` field to `score_classification`, have the server run
  `voteDimension` itself, and record the real `agreement` string. Then the MCP path produces the
  same `2/3` / `3/3` artefacts as the batch path and the protocol is genuinely one protocol.

### 3.3 UX & Design (20 %)

The visual language (`globals.css`, 669 lines — dark, single amber accent, no red/green, band word
always printed beside the number) is disciplined and will read as considered. Real problems:

- **Nothing on the market page says what a score means relative to others.** A judge sees "78 / 100
  high" with no scale, no distribution, no comparison. The score index has ten markets; show the
  contract's position on the strip.
- **`Level 1 · natural_process`** — raw enum labels leak into the UI
  (`market/[marketId]/page.tsx:76`). `natural_process`, `prohibited_enforced`, `large_or_unbounded`
  are internal identifiers.
- **Five identical evidence quotes** on every live market page (§1.5).
- **`insufficient_info` renders as `Level  · `** when `level` is null but `insufficientInfo` is
  false (probe C) — reachable from any MCP host.
- **The opaque-contract result is 75/high** (probe D). On the incoming `/assess` page, a judge who
  pastes a one-line question — the most likely first interaction — gets *"high risk"* driven
  entirely by five conservative defaults. The caveats explain it, but the number is what is read.
  **Needed:** a distinct "undeterminable" presentation. When ≥3 dimensions are `insufficient_info`,
  suppress the number and show *"Not enough contract text to assess — this contract states N of 5
  structural properties"*, with the missing ones named. That turns the framework's biggest UX
  liability into a demonstration of intellectual honesty, and it is the single best thing the
  playground can do.
- **The low-band summary can contradict the dimensions below it** (§1.5).
- **No empty/error state for a stale cache.** `readScoreIndex` returns
  `{ generatedAt: 1970-01-01, markets: [] }` on a missing file (`scores.ts:16`) and the page renders
  "No live markets scored yet." with no explanation.

### 3.4 Business & Ecosystem Impact (20 %)

**The biggest narrative risk in the submission: eight of ten scored markets do not exist on
DreamDEX.** A judge sees "Curated risk spectrum" — CPI, FOMC, elections, celebrities, airstrikes —
none of which DreamDEX lists, and reasonably concludes the project was designed for Polymarket and
pointed at DreamDEX for the hackathon.

The framing that pre-empts it must be explicit, on the landing page and in the video's first 20
seconds, and it should be an argument *for* DreamDEX rather than a defence:

> DreamDEX today lists BTC/ETH price binaries — which this framework scores at 3/100, the safest
> structure a prediction market can have. That is a competitive fact, not a limitation: it is the
> reason a new venue can list non-price contracts *with* a published integrity standard from day
> one, instead of retrofitting one after the first scandal. The curated set is not a wish list; it
> is the risk map for the categories DreamDEX has to underwrite next, and LevelField is the
> instrument that says which of them can be listed safely and which need a disclosure rule first.

Concretely, the strongest Business-Impact asset available before submission is a **listing-guidance
artefact**: take the anchor library, run it over the 10–20 market templates a venue would plausibly
list next (sports, awards, macro, corporate, governance), and publish a table of
score / recommended-listing-condition (e.g. *"D5 ≥ 4 and D3 ≥ 4 → require a settlement delay or do
not list"*). That is a product DreamDEX can use on Monday, it is unique among 52 entries, and it
makes "expand the ecosystem" literal rather than aspirational. The in-flight curated expansion to
~16 contracts is 80 % of the work already.

Second: the **venue-comparison feature** falls out of the D3 fix in §2.2/#7 — the same contract
scored for DreamDEX vs a regulated venue vs an offshore venue. That is directly "attract new users"
and it is one input field away.

Third: the agent story is the strongest ecosystem argument and is currently under-sold. Organizers
explicitly welcome AI-agent developers; an MCP server that any trading agent can consult before
placing an order is *infrastructure for other people's entries*. The in-flight
`scripts/agent-demo.ts` should be framed exactly that way — "your bot calls one tool before it
trades" — and its output should be in the README as a transcript.

### 3.5 Presentation & Demo (15 %)

- **What the demo cannot currently show live:** an actual DreamDEX market being scored *now* (§1.3
  picks month-old rows); any on-chain artefact (registry is in flight); inter-run stability (nothing
  produces it); the injection defence working *as a mechanism* rather than as a hand-authored
  fixture (§1.2(a)).
- **Injection demo-ability, specifically.** Today the demo is: open
  `data/curated/injection-test.json`, note the embedded instruction, open the market page, observe
  90/high and the notice banner. A judge who asks *"what stopped it?"* gets: a sentence in the
  system prompt, and a boolean the classifier set on itself. There is no executable moment.
  **Make it executable in ~2 hours:** add a server-side lexical scanner in `packages/scoring`
  (a small regex set over the rendered contract text: `classify (all )?(dimensions|D[1-5])`,
  `level \d`, `ignore (any |all )?(previous|prior) instructions`, `automated (risk )?assessor`,
  `system note`, `you are`, `pre-?audited`). Then in `score_classification` and the batch scorer:
  (i) set `instructionLikeContentDetected` **server-side**, never trusting the caller;
  (ii) reject any `evidence_quote` that overlaps a flagged span, with the same structured error the
  verbatim check uses. Now the demo is: paste an injected contract into `/assess`, the server rejects
  the host's compliant classification and names the offending span, and the second attempt scores
  90. That is a 30-second video beat and it converts the README's strongest claim from false to true.
- **Reproducibility beat.** `score:all` twice → an 11-line timestamp-only diff is a genuinely good
  15 seconds of video and costs nothing to film.
- **`FEEDBACK.md` is the most differentiated deliverable in the repo** — 9 entries, all evidence-
  backed, several with 3/3 sampled counter-examples. It is also a *required* submission item that
  most entries will treat as a formality. Give it a title page, an executive summary of the four
  entries that block third-party tooling (#1 no description text, #4 undocumented indexer,
  #6 broken `oracleQuestionId`, #9 no venue registry), and a one-line "impact on our build" per
  entry. Two hours, and it is the single easiest place to be visibly better than 51 other teams.
- **Stale README numbers** (38 tests vs 46) and a status checklist with three unchecked boxes will
  be read literally.

### 3.6 The MCP protocol from a cold-start agent's view (walked tool-by-tool)

Executed against the running server. What an agent host actually sees:

1. **`tools/list`** returns three tools. Their descriptions are unusually good — the
   `get_assessment_protocol` description states outright that *"The CALLER's own model reads this
   and performs the classification itself"* and names the next call. An agent that reads only the
   list can form a correct plan. Nothing here is misleading.
2. **The failure mode is that step 1 is skippable.** `score_classification`'s input schema is fully
   self-describing (D1–D5, level 1–5, labels, quotes). A capable model can fill it in *without ever
   calling `get_assessment_protocol`* — inventing its own notion of what level 3 means. The server
   accepts it and stamps `promptVersion` (`server.ts:217`) as if the anchor library had been read.
   **The `promptVersion` in an MCP-produced ScoreResult is not evidence of anything.** Fix: have
   `get_assessment_protocol` return a short `protocol_token` (hash of the rendered prompt) and
   require it on `score_classification`; reject with a structured error pointing at
   `get_assessment_protocol`. Ten lines, and it makes `promptVersion` mean what the README says.
3. **`get_assessment_protocol` returns 11,277 characters in one text block.** That is a large,
   uncacheable context cost per assessment, and there is no way to fetch just one dimension. Fine
   for a one-shot; expensive for the "agent checks every market before trading" story the project
   wants to tell. Consider a `dimension?: "D1"` parameter and a compact mode.
4. **The verbatim-quote error is excellent.** `evidence_quote_not_verbatim` names the failing
   dimensions and the exact offending strings, and says "Not scored". This is the best-designed
   surface in the repo and a self-correcting agent recovers on the next call without guessing.
5. **`list_anchor_library`** returns 12,656 characters of JSON — useful for audit, redundant with
   tool 1 for classification. No cost to keep.
6. **Missing for a cold-start agent:** there is no way to ask *"score DreamDEX market 0x…"* in one
   call, and no way to discover what has already been scored. The in-flight `assess_market` /
   `list_scored_markets` close exactly this. When they land, make `assess_market` the *first* tool
   in the list and have its description say "start here" — cold-start agents pick the first
   plausible tool.
7. **No tool annotations.** None of the three declares `readOnlyHint`/`openWorldHint`. Hosts that
   gate on annotations will prompt unnecessarily. Trivial to add.
